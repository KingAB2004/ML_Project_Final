ALTERNATIVE WAYS TO MAKE THE SUPPORTER LESS INTRUSIVE (compared with our gate + four agents)

E1. Activation steering
   Why:     controls intrusiveness inside the model at run time, with no training and no extra calls - the
            cheapest alternative to our gate.
   Done:    contrastive activation addition: a "gentle minus intrusive" direction from 400 training-split contexts
            (the same context read under a gentle and an intrusive instruction), added to the residual stream of
            cell A's one-call listener. Layer and strength chosen on 20 calibration people by a rule fixed before
            the sweep (layer 12, strength 0.2); final run multi-session on the test people against an unsteered
            control with the same people and session seeds.
   Result:  IP -0.008 [-0.013, -0.003] (paired), Success and PRI unchanged, replies shorter (27.5 vs 31.8 words);
            does not survive Holm. Layer 16 cut IP more but cost 0.06 Success, so the rule rejected it.
   Where:   R/steering/  ·  S/15_activation_steering/  ·  code src/steering.py, scripts/steering.sh,
            --steer in src/evaluate.py, hook in src/llm.py

E2. Preference alignment (DPO)
   Why:     puts "do not be intrusive" into the weights instead of a gate.
   Done:    the fine-tuned listener wrote 4 replies to each of 1,200 training-split contexts; the base Qwen critic
            (not the evaluation judge, so the model cannot learn to please its own judge) scored each on IP;
            least vs most intrusive reply = a pair when they differ by >= 1 scale point and are within 1.5x in
            length -> 460 pairs. DPO on the SFT adapter (beta 0.1, 1 epoch). Final run multi-session on the test
            people against the SFT model, same people and session seeds.
   Result:  IP -0.027 [-0.033, -0.021] (paired, Holm), the largest IP drop of any method; Success +0.009 and PRI
            -0.001 (both n.s.); counterfactual PRI +0.005 -> -0.000; replies shorter (23 vs 28 words); no extra
            calls. Held-out pair accuracy was only 0.52 (23 pairs), so the training signal was weak - the behaviour
            changed anyway.
   Where:   R/preference/ (pairs, candidates, training log; the adapter weights are not in git)
            ·  S/18_preference_alignment_dpo/  ·  code src/preference.py, scripts/preference.sh, --tag in evaluate.py

E3. Cost per turn
   Why:     the methods differ in how many model calls they need; the SOP's recovery-vs-cost idea extended to all.
   Done:    model calls per supporter turn counted from each pipeline's structure and logged revisions.
   Result:  cell A 1.0, cell B 3.3, cell C 4.0, cell D 5.4, step 12 4.3, steering 1.0, DPO 1.0 calls per turn.
            DPO and steering lower IP at no extra cost; the gate costs 2-4 extra calls per turn.
   Where:   S/24_cost_per_turn/  ·  code scripts/cost_per_turn.py

LLM PSYCHOLOGY PROBES (what making the supporter less intrusive does to its other behaviour)
All judged on 10.10.10.104 (see E7 for why that matters).

E4. Sycophancy
   Why:     a supporter told not to be intrusive might simply agree with everything, including distorted or risky
            beliefs.
   Done:    48 hand-written seeker turns in 8 categories (catastrophizing, mind reading, overgeneralizing,
            self-labeling, self-blame, all-or-nothing, feeling-as-fact, risky plans such as stopping medication);
            every system replies once; the judge scores sycophancy (agrees with the distortion), balance (validates
            the feeling, not the belief) and harshness, plus IP.
   Result:  no system turns sycophantic: sycophancy 0.16-0.19 for every system (base 0.170, steered 0.174, SFT 0.179,
            DPO 0.188, cell D 0.163, step 12 0.171), balance ~0.88-0.90, no harshness. The exception is risky
            plans (e.g. skipping meals, stopping medication): SFT 0.32 and steered 0.24 go along with them more
            than base 0.17. Lower intrusiveness did not buy more agreement with distorted beliefs.
   Where:   R/psych/sycophancy/  ·  S/19_llm_psychology/  ·  data/psych/sycophancy_probes.jsonl,
            prompts/metric_syco.md, code src/psych.py

E5. Emotional intelligence (EmoBench)
   Why:     does fine-tuning, steering or DPO change measured emotional understanding?
   Done:    the English half of EmoBench (Sabour et al., ACL 2024; MIT): 200 scenarios asked for the emotion and its
            cause, 200 for the most effective response; answered from letter probabilities.
   Result:  base 0.637, steered 0.630, SFT 0.610, DPO 0.610 (CIs overlap): fine-tuning costs a little measured
            emotional intelligence, steering and DPO change nothing further.
   Where:   R/psych/emobench/  ·  S/19_*  ·  data/external/emobench/

E6. Dependence and boundaries
   Why:     AI-companion safety: does the supporter build reliance on itself, or point the person to the people in
            their life and to outside help, and does that drift across sessions?
   Done:    phrase counts over every generated supporter turn (the gate's fallback template, which says "I'm here",
            excluded), and the judge on whole sessions of the first 15 test people of each arm (reliance, outward
            support, autonomy).
   Result:  reliance on the supporter is the same everywhere (~0.26-0.30, no drift from session 1 to 3+). What
            differs is pointing outward: cell D 0.44 and need-state memory 0.40 vs cell A 0.60, with less autonomy
            too (0.72, 0.69 vs 0.86) - the four-agent and memory systems keep the conversation on the supporter.
            Steering, SFT and DPO stay close to cell A (outward 0.56-0.59). Mentions of friends, family or
            professionals are rare in every arm (< 2 % of turns).
   Where:   R/psych/dependence/  ·  S/19_*  ·  prompts/metric_dependence.md

CHECKS ON THE EVALUATION ITSELF

E7. Second judge and the judge's serving machine
   Why:     the headline numbers come from one judge model; without human ratings, at least check they do not hang on
            that judge.
   Done:    (a) Llama-3.1-8B rescoring IP / PRI / Success on 9 key arms; (b) the same items judged by the same Mistral
            weights served by Ollama 0.35 (lab) and 0.18 (10.10.10.104).
   Result:  (a) every IP conclusion keeps its sign under Llama (gate, decomposition, steering, step 12) except the
            need-state memory IP rise, which Llama does not reproduce; per-item agreement rho: IP 0.36, PRI 0.63,
            Success 0.20 (Llama's Success sits near the floor, so it cannot check Success).
            (b) the serving machine matters. Same items, same weights: session-level scales shift a lot (cell A
            reliance 0.08 on the lab vs 0.27 on 10.10.10.104; base-model sycophancy 0.02 vs 0.17), and turn-level
            IP shifts a little (cell A 0.068 lab vs 0.079 on 10.10.10.104, item rho 0.66; the lab against itself
            two weeks apart: 0.071, rho 0.90). So all psychology probes were re-judged on 10.10.10.104, and the
            one headline IP comparison that mixed machines (ExTES vs ours on our people) was re-judged on one
            machine (S/16).
   Where:   R/*/scores_judge2/  ·  S/21_judge_judge_agreement/  ·  S/25_judge_machine_check/
            ·  code scripts/judge_agreement.py, scripts/judge_machine_check.py, --judge-role in src/metrics.py

E8. Multiple-comparison correction
   Why:     dozens of confidence intervals are reported; some would look significant by chance.
   Done:    42 headline contrasts, bootstrap p over people (paired where arms share seeds), Holm at 0.05.
   Result:  9 survive: gate with four agents (IP), decomposition (IP, twice), proactive vs reactive seeker (IP),
            step 12 vs cell D (IP, PRI), the transformers-vs-Ollama backend check (IP), ExTES-trained vs ours
            (Success on ExTES people), DPO vs SFT (IP).
   Where:   S/23_multiple_comparisons/  ·  code scripts/multiple_comparisons.py

E9. Backend check
   Why:     step 12, steering and DPO had to run on transformers (Ollama cannot load adapters or hooks), cells A-D on
            Ollama.
   Done:    the untuned one-call listener on both backends (steering control vs cell A).
   Result:  transformers raises IP by +0.017 [0.009, 0.025] (Holm). Comparisons across backends (step 12 vs cell D)
            carry this confound; steering and DPO are compared with controls on their own backend.
   Where:   S/23_*  ·  S/20_step12_finetuned_generator/

E10. Qualitative examples
   Why:     the report needs real cases, chosen so they illustrate the numbers rather than argue for them.
   Done:    fixed selection rules: gate revisions and fallbacks, steered vs unsteered replies to the same seeker turn,
            risky-plan replies, the turns that provoked the most resistance, need-state memory handing session 2 its
            brief; plus a data note: Qwen drifts into Chinese in at most ~1 % of turns.
   Where:   S/22_qualitative_examples/examples.md  ·  code scripts/qualitative_examples.py
